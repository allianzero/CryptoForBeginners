# CryptoForBeginners

The easiest starting point for crypto: what it is, how it works, and how to start safely. No prior knowledge is assumed, and no step here requires you to spend money before you understand it.

> This guide is educational only, not financial advice. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and never share your seed phrase with anyone.

## 1. What is crypto, in plain language

Cryptocurrency is digital money that runs on a public ledger called a **blockchain** instead of a bank's private database.

- A **blockchain** is a shared record of transactions, copied across many computers. Once a transaction is recorded and confirmed, it is very hard to change or delete.
- A **coin** is the native asset of its own blockchain. Examples: BTC on Bitcoin, ETH on Ethereum, BNB on BNB Chain.
- A **token** is an asset created by a smart contract on someone else's blockchain. Most tokens you meet are built on Ethereum, BNB Chain, or a similar chain.
- A **wallet** does not store coins inside your phone. Coins live on the blockchain. Your wallet stores the **keys** that prove you own them and let you move them.

The most important idea in crypto is simple: **whoever controls the keys controls the coins.**

## 2. The three things every beginner meets

### Wallets
A wallet is your keychain. There are two kinds:

- **Custodial wallet:** an exchange holds your keys for you. You log in with email and password, like a bank app. Easy to recover, but the exchange can freeze or lose your funds.
- **Non-custodial wallet:** you hold your own keys, usually as a 12- or 24-word **seed phrase**. Nobody can freeze it, but nobody can recover it for you either.

### Exchanges
An exchange is a marketplace where you buy and sell crypto. A **centralised exchange (CEX)** is run by a company. A **decentralised exchange (DEX)** is run by smart contracts and lets you trade directly from your own wallet.

### Networks
Crypto lives on a specific network, and the network matters. Sending an asset on the wrong network is one of the most common beginner mistakes, and it is usually irreversible. Always check the network before you send anything.

## 3. How a transaction works

A simple transaction has five parts:

1. **Sender:** the wallet that sends the asset.
2. **Receiver:** the wallet address that receives it.
3. **Asset and amount:** what is sent, and how much.
4. **Network fee (gas):** a small payment to the network for processing the transaction. You pay it in the network's native coin.
5. **Confirmation:** the network records the transaction on the blockchain. More confirmations mean it is more final.

A transaction, once confirmed, generally **cannot be reversed**. There is no bank to call. That is why checking the address, network, and amount before you press send is not optional — it is the whole safety system.

## 4. Your first steps, in order

Follow this order. Do not skip the test steps.

1. **Learn before you buy.** Read this guide and the wallet and security guides in this collection first.
2. **Set up 2FA.** On every exchange account and on your email, turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app.
3. **Create a wallet.** Write your seed phrase on paper, by hand. Never photograph it, never store it in a cloud note, never share it.
4. **Buy a small amount.** Start with an amount so small that losing it would not hurt.
5. **Do a test withdrawal.** Move a tiny amount to your own wallet and confirm it arrives before moving more.
6. **Do a test send.** Send a tiny amount between your own wallets and confirm it, so you practise checking addresses and networks.
7. **Only then consider larger amounts.** Slowly, and only with money you can afford to lose.

## 5. Gas fees, explained simply

A gas fee is what you pay the network to process your transaction. It is not a fee charged by your wallet app, and it is not the price of the coin you are sending.

- Every network has its own fee token: ETH on Ethereum, BNB on BNB Chain.
- Fees rise when the network is busy and fall when it is quiet.
- A failed transaction can still cost gas, because the network still did the work of trying.
- Always keep a small amount of the network's native coin in your wallet for gas. A wallet full of tokens but with zero native coin cannot send anything.

## 6. Common beginner mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Sending on the wrong network | Funds go to an address on a network the receiver does not use, often lost | Match the network on both sides, every time |
| Typing an address by hand | One wrong character sends funds to a stranger | Copy and paste, then compare the first and last characters |
| Sharing a seed phrase | Anyone with it can take everything, instantly | Never share it, with anyone, for any reason |
| Clicking a link from a DM | You land on a fake site that steals your login or keys | Only use bookmarks or the official app |
| Buying because of hype | You buy at the top of a pump, then watch it fall | Understand what a project does before you buy |
| Approving unlimited spending | A malicious contract can drain your tokens later | Approve only what you need, and revoke old approvals |

## 7. How to spot a scam

Stop and verify if you see any of these together:

- A promise of guaranteed returns, doubling, or "free money for connecting your wallet".
- Urgency: "act now or lose it", "last chance", "your account will be closed".
- A request for your seed phrase, password, or a wallet approval you do not understand.
- A support agent who contacts you first by DM.
- A token that appeared in your wallet and whose website asks you to connect and "claim" it.

Real projects do not double your money for sending it to them. Real support never asks for your seed phrase.

## 8. Safety checklist

- [ ] 2FA on your exchange account and email.
- [ ] Seed phrase written on paper, stored offline, never photographed.
- [ ] Bookmarks for the real exchange and wallet sites you use.
- [ ] Test transaction done before any larger send.
- [ ] Address, network, and amount checked before every send.
- [ ] Old token approvals reviewed and revoked when no longer needed.
- [ ] No password reused between crypto accounts.

## 9. Frequently asked questions

**Is crypto anonymous?**
Mostly no. Most blockchains are pseudonymous: your address is public even if your name is not attached to it, and transactions can often be traced.

**Can I get crypto back if I send it to the wrong address?**
Almost always no. That is why test transactions matter so much.

**Do I need a hardware wallet?**
Not on day one. Consider one once the amount you hold would hurt to lose, because it keeps your keys offline.

**Is Bitcoin the same as crypto?**
No. Bitcoin is one cryptocurrency. "Crypto" is the whole category, including thousands of coins, tokens, and networks.

**How much should I start with?**
An amount you can afford to lose completely, and small enough that a mistake is a lesson, not a disaster.
